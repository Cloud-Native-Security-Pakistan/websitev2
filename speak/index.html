// what-we-look-for

Real work beats hot takes.

No vendor pitches, no 40-slide introduction to what Kubernetes is. Bring the demo, the incident, or the config that actually ran.

> Demos over decks

A live cluster, a runnable repo, a terminal you type in. Talk is cheap — show the kubectl.

> Pakistani context

The constraints you actually work under: local scale, local budgets, local regulators. Global topics are welcome; grounded ones land hardest.

> Honest postmortems

The outage, the misconfiguration, the CVE that shipped. What broke, what you learned, what you would do again.

> One clear takeaway

The single thing the room can apply on Monday morning. One is enough.

// review-sla

You will always know where you stand.

Three checkpoints. No silent rejections, and no proposal sits in a queue without an answer.

≤ 3 working days

Acknowledged

A human confirms your proposal arrived. If you hear nothing in three working days, something broke — resend it and say so.

≤ 7 days

Reviewed

The programme committee reads every proposal. We may ask a clarifying question or suggest a sharper angle before deciding.

≤ 10 days

Answered

Accepted, held for a later session, or a "not this one, here is why". Every submitter gets a written answer either way.

// terms

What we do with your submission.

  • Your email is used for this proposal only. Submitting does not subscribe you to anything, and we add no one to a mailing list from this form.
  • No automated follow-ups. No reminder sequences, no nudge emails, no direct messages. A person replies, once, with a decision.
  • Your abstract stays yours. We publish the title, abstract, and your name only after you accept a slot, and only as you wrote them.
  • A rejection is a real answer. We tell you why, and a "no" for one session is not a "no" for the next.
  • Slots are not for sale. Sponsorship never buys a talk slot — see sponsorship for what backing an event does and does not include.

Speakers and attendees are bound by the Code of Conduct. Not here to speak? Join the community instead.
