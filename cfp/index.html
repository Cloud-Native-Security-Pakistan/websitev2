// what-we-look-for

Real work beats hot takes.

No vendor pitches. No 40-slide intros to "what is Kubernetes." We want the demo, the incident, the config that actually ran in production.

> Demos over decks

A live cluster, a runnable repo, a terminal you actually type in. Talk is cheap — show the kubectl.

> Pakistani context

SBP guidelines, local fintech scale, the constraints you actually work under. Global topics welcome too — grounded ones land hardest.

> Honest postmortems

The outage, the misconfig, the CVE that shipped. What broke, what you learned, what you'd do again.

> One clear takeaway

The single thing the room can apply Monday morning. If a talk has one, it's a good talk.

// review-timeline

You'll always know where you stand.

No black holes. Here's exactly what happens after you hit submit.

≤ 3 working days

Submission acknowledged

A human confirms we received your proposal. If you hear nothing in three working days, something broke — resend it and say so.

≤ 7 days

Committee review

The CFP committee reads every proposal. We may ask a clarifying question or suggest a sharper angle.

≤ 10 days

Decision

Accepted, held for a later session, or a "not this one, here's why." Every submitter gets a written answer — no silent rejections.

// first-time-speakers

Never spoken before? Perfect.

If you have the substance but not the slides, an organizer works through it with you: we help you scope the talk, shape the demo, and run a dry run in front of a small, friendly room before you present it to the chapter.

You bring the work. We bring the runway. No speaking experience required — just something real you have done with cloud-native security.

Want that help? Say so in your submission, or email hi@cloudnativesecurity.pk.

// terms

What we do with your submission.

  • Your email is used for this proposal only. Submitting does not subscribe you to anything, and we add no one to a mailing list from this form.
  • No automated follow-ups. No reminder sequences, no nudge emails, no direct messages. A person replies, once, with a decision.
  • Your abstract stays yours. We publish the title, abstract, and your name only after you accept a slot, and only as you wrote them.
  • A rejection is a real answer. We tell you why, and a "no" for one cycle is not a "no" for the next.
  • Slots are not for sale. Sponsorship never buys a talk slot — see sponsorship for what backing an event does and does not include.

Speakers and attendees are bound by the Code of Conduct. Not here to speak? Join the community instead.
