On 3 June 2026 the European Commission adopted its Tech Sovereignty Package — and open source sits at the center of it. Not charity. Not a nice-to-have. Strategy.

The dependency Europe is done with.

80%+ of the EU's digital products, services and IP come from outside the bloc. Three US clouds — AWS, Azure, Google Cloud — control ~70% of the market. And roughly €264 billion a year goes to US cloud software.

That's the structural lock-in the new Tech Sovereignty Package is built to break — with open, interoperable infrastructure as the lever.

What's actually inside the EU Tech Sovereignty Package:

→ EU Open Source Strategy — make open digital assets easy to discover, reuse and maintain, with a push for the public sector to adopt open source.
→ Cloud and AI Development Act (CADA) — aims to roughly triple EU cloud capacity over 5–7 years and keep critical sectors sovereign.
→ Chips Act 2.0 — drive demand for European-made semiconductors.
→ Strategic Roadmap for Digitalisation and AI in Energy.

Four moves, one goal: reduce structural dependency and build open, interoperable infrastructure.
